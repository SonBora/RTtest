Thoracic Cage Survey

Comprehensive Radiographic Evaluation of the Ribs, Sternum & Thoracic Skeleton

Position Images

Examination Information

The general Thoracic Cage survey encompasses combined projections of the ribs, sternum, and thoracic articulation to assess extensive blunt trauma, osseous metastasis, metabolic bone disorders, or congenital chest wall deformities.

Body Part Thoracic Cage / Bony Thorax
Projection AP / PA Thoracic Survey Routine
IR Size 35 × 43 cm (Lengthwise)
SID 100 cm – 180 cm
kVp 70 – 85
mAs 12 – 25

Patient Position

• Patient positioned erect facing vertical Bucky (or supine if trauma prevents standing).

• Center mid-sagittal plane (MSP) or affected hemi-thorax to grid midline.

• Adjust shoulders in same transverse plane and roll shoulders forward to clear scapulae.

• Place top of IR approximately 4 cm above shoulder level.

• Respiration: Full inspiration for upper thoracic cage evaluation; full expiration for lower thoracic cage. Suspended during exposure.

Central Ray

• Central Ray perpendicular to Image Receptor.

• Center Central Ray to level of T7 (approx. 18–20 cm below vertebra prominens / mid-sternum).

• Center IR to CR.

• Collimate to cover entire thoracic cage margins including lateral rib borders and sternoclavicular joints.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Full bony thorax visualized including bilateral rib structures, sternum, and costal cartilages.

✓ No rotation: distance from vertebral column to lateral rib borders equal bilaterally.

✓ Optimal density and contrast demonstrating bony trabeculae and cortical margins clearly.

✓ Absence of motion blurring on ribs and thoracic articulation.
