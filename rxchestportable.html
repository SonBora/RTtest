Anteroposterior Bedside / Mobile Chest Radiographic Projection

Position Images

Examination Information

The AP portable chest projection is performed at the patient's bedside for critically ill, non-ambulatory, or postoperative patients. It evaluates pulmonary pathology, line/tube placements (ET tube, CVP line), and cardiac size.

Body Part Chest / Thorax
Projection Anteroposterior (AP) Portable
IR Size 35 × 43 cm
SID 100 – 180 cm (Minimum 100 cm)
kVp 80 – 90 (or 110–120 with grid)
mAs 2 – 4

Patient Position

• Patient seated erect or semi-erect in bed as much as tolerated (supine if condition requires).

• Place Image Receptor behind patient's back, aligned with top of IR approx. 4–5 cm above shoulders.

• Roll shoulders forward and rotate arms internally to move scapulae out of lung fields.

• Mid-sagittal plane (MSP) centered perpendicular to IR.

• Exposure made on full inspiration (or at peak of breath in ventilated patients).

Central Ray

• Central Ray angled caudad to be perpendicular to long axis of sternum (prevents clavicular overlap).

• Center Central Ray to mid-sternum, level of T7 (approx. 8–10 cm below jugular notch).

• Ensure CR is perpendicular to plane of Image Receptor.

• Collimate on four sides to outer chest margins.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Entire lung fields included from apices down to costophrenic angles.

✓ Adequate inspiration shown (preferably 8–10 posterior ribs above diaphragm).

✓ Clavicles projected in upper lung fields above 3rd ribs.

✓ No rotation: sternoclavicular joints equidistant from vertebral spine.

✓ Clear visualization of endotracheal tube, central venous catheters, or medical lines if present.
