Ribs AP (Lower / Below Diaphragm)

Anteroposterior Radiographic Projection for Lower Ribs (Ribs 8–12)

Position Images

Examination Information

The AP projection for lower ribs is performed to demonstrate pathology or fractures involving the posterior lower thoracic cage (8th through 12th ribs). It is typically exposed on full expiration in a recumbent position to push the diaphragm upward and maximize contrast against abdominal tissues.

Body Part Ribs / Thoracic Cage
Projection Anteroposterior (AP) Lower Ribs
IR Size 35 × 43 cm (Crosswise or Lengthwise)
SID 100 cm
kVp 75 – 85
mAs 12 – 25

Patient Position

• Patient supine / recumbent on table (allows abdominal organs to push diaphragm up).

• Align mid-sagittal plane (MSP) or affected side to midline of grid/table.

• Arms placed comfortably at sides or resting above head out of the ROI.

• Lower edge of Image Receptor placed at level of iliac crest.

• Exposure made on full expiration (to raise diaphragm above 8th-9th ribs).

Central Ray

• Central Ray perpendicular to Image Receptor.

• Center Central Ray halfway between xiphoid process and lower rib cage (approx. level of T12 / L1).

• Center IR to CR (bottom of IR roughly at iliac crests).

• Collimate on four sides to include 8th through 12th ribs bilaterally or unilaterally as indicated.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ 8th through 12th posterior ribs clearly visible below the diaphragm.

✓ Rib bony detail and cortical margins sharply demonstrated without motion blur.

✓ No patient rotation: distance from spine to lateral rib margins equal on both sides (unless unilateral exam).

✓ Optimal exposure factors ensuring visualization of ribs through dense abdominal structures.
