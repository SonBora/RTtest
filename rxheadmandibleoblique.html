Mandible Oblique

Lateral Oblique Projection (Body, Ramus, Angle)

Position Images

Examination Information

The Mandible Oblique (Lateral Oblique) projection is used to demonstrate specific portions of the mandible (ramus, body, or mentum) to diagnose fractures, pathologies, or impacted teeth without overlapping the opposite side.

Body Part Mandibular Body / Ramus / Angle
Projection Lateral Oblique
IR Size 24 × 30 cm
SID 100 cm
kVp 70 - 75
mAs 10 - 15

Patient Position

• Patient seated erect or semi-prone with head placed in true lateral position, then rotated.

• Rotate head toward IR based on area of interest: 15° for Ramus, 30° for Body, 45° for Mentum/Symphysis.

• Extend the neck slightly to prevent superimposition of the mandible over the cervical spine.

• Rest the affected side of the face close to or against the IR surface.

• Immobilize head to avoid motion blur during exposure.

Central Ray

• CR directed 25° cephalad to exit the region of interest.

• Center CR to the area of the mandibular body, angle, or ramus depending on clinical focus.

• Center IR to CR.

• Collimate tightly around the region of interest to improve contrast and reduce scatter radiation.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Target area of the mandible clearly visualized without superimposition from the opposite jaw side.

✓ Mandibular body and angle free from overlap with the cervical vertebrae.

✓ Ramus, condyle, and coronoid process demonstrated clearly (for ramus projection).

✓ Optimal exposure factors showing distinct trabecular patterns and cortical margins.

✓ Proper side marker placed accurately without obscuring anatomy.
