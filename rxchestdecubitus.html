Chest AP Lateral Decubitus

Chest AP Projection - Lateral Decubitus Position (Right or Left Lateral Decubitus)

Position Images

Examination Information

The chest lateral decubitus projection is crucial for detecting small amounts of pleural effusion (down side) or air in the pleural space / pneumothorax (up side) when the patient cannot stand erect.

Body Part Chest / Thorax
Projection AP Projection (Right / Left Lateral Decubitus)
IR Size 35 × 43 cm
SID 180 cm
kVp 110 – 125
mAs 4 – 8

Patient Position

• Patient lying on side (Right or Left Lateral Decubitus) on a radiolucent pad for at least 5 minutes prior to exposure.

• Elevate body 5–8 cm off table if examining dependent side for fluid.

• Place back against vertical IR grid, flex knees slightly for stability.

• Raise both arms above head to clear lung fields.

• Mid-sagittal plane (MSP) perpendicular to IR; mid-coronal plane parallel to IR.

• Exposure made on end of second full inspiration.

Central Ray

• Central Ray horizontal and perpendicular to IR.

• Center Central Ray to level of T7 (approx. 8–10 cm below jugular notch).

• Center IR to CR (top of IR approx. 4–5 cm above shoulders).

• Collimate on four sides to outer chest margins.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Entire lung fields included from apices to costophrenic angles.

✓ Air-fluid levels or pneumothorax clearly demonstrated.

✓ No rotation: sternoclavicular joints equidistant from spine, equal distance from vertebral column to lateral rib margins.

✓ Arms elevated sufficiently to prevent arm soft tissue over lung fields.

✓ Proper exposure technique showing faint outline of thoracic vertebrae and vascular markings throughout lungs.
