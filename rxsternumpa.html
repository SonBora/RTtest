Sternum PA Oblique (RAO)

Posteroanterior Oblique Radiographic Projection of the Sternum (Right Anterior Oblique Position)

Position Images

Examination Information

The RAO position projects the sternum over the homogenous shadow of the heart, shifting dense thoracic vertebrae out of the field of view. It is used to evaluate sternal fractures, osteomyelitis, and inflammatory or arthritic changes.

Body Part Sternum / Thoracic Cage
Projection PA Oblique (RAO Position)
IR Size 24 × 30 cm (Lengthwise)
SID 100 cm
kVp 70 – 80
mAs 25 – 40 (Breathing Technique)

Patient Position

• Patient standing erect or prone facing vertical grid / bucky.

• Rotate body 15 to 20 degrees into Right Anterior Oblique (RAO) position (shallower rotation for thick thorax, greater rotation for thin thorax).

• Place right arm down by side; left arm flexed above head holding grid frame for balance.

• Align long axis of sternum to midline of grid.

• Respiration: Low mA with long exposure time (3–4 seconds) during shallow breathing (orthostatic technique) to blur pulmonary vascular markings.

Central Ray

• Central Ray perpendicular to Image Receptor.

• Center Central Ray to mid-sternum (level of T7, approx. 8–10 cm below jugular notch, entering elevated left side of thorax).

• Center IR to CR (top of IR approx. 4 cm above jugular notch).

• Collimate tightly to long narrow strip over sternum.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Entire sternum (manubrium, body, and xiphoid process) clearly demonstrated over heart shadow.

✓ Correct degree of rotation: sternum projected free from superimposition of thoracic spine.

✓ Pulmonary vascular markings blurred by orthostatic breathing technique.

✓ Sharp bony detail of sternal borders and sternoclavicular joints without motion blur of sternum.
