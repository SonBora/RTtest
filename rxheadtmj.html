TMJ View

Temporomandibular Joint Projection (Open & Closed Mouth)

Position Images

Examination Information

The Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) projection demonstrates the relationship between the mandibular condyle and the mandibular fossa in both closed-mouth and open-mouth positions to diagnose dislocations, joint dysfunction, and fractures.

Body Part Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ)
Projection Axiolateral / Oblique Lateral (Open & Closed)
IR Size 18 × 24 cm
SID 100 cm
kVp 70 - 75
mAs 12 - 20

Patient Position

• Patient seated erect or semi-prone with affected side against IR in lateral head position.

• Align Mid-sagittal Plane (MSP) parallel to IR plane; Interpupillary Line (IPL) perpendicular to IR.

• Perform initial projection with **Mouth Closed** (teeth together).

• Perform second projection with **Mouth Open** wide (unless contraindicated by trauma).

• Immobilize head during exposure to maintain true lateral position.

Central Ray

• CR directed 15° to 30° caudad (typically 25°–30° caudad for Modified Schüller Method).

• Center CR to enter approximately 7.5 cm (3 inches) superior to the upside EAM (External Auditory Meatus).

• Exit through downside TMJ (TMJ closest to the IR).

• Center IR to CR; collimate closely to include only the TMJ region.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Condyle demonstrated within mandibular fossa on Closed-Mouth image.

✓ Condyle moves inferiorly and anteriorly to the articular eminence on Open-Mouth image.

✓ Downside TMJ clearly demonstrated without superimposition of the opposite TMJ.

✓ Proper labeling of "Closed" and "Open" positions on radiographs.

✓ High contrast with sharp bony margins of mandibular fossa and condyle.
