Chest PA Expiration

Posteroanterior Chest Radiographic Projection (Full Expiration View)

Position Images

Examination Information

The PA expiration chest projection is typically compared with a standard inspiration PA chest view. It is primarily used to detect a small pneumothorax, foreign body aspiration, vocal cord paralysis, or diaphragm movement/excursion.

Body Part Chest / Thorax
Projection Posteroanterior (PA) Expiration
IR Size 35 × 43 cm
SID 180 cm
kVp 110 – 125
mAs 4 – 8

Patient Position

• Patient standing erect facing the chest bucky / vertical IR.

• Chin elevated, resting against top of IR support.

• Place backs of hands low on hips, roll shoulders forward to move scapulae out of lung fields.

• Mid-sagittal plane (MSP) centered and perpendicular to IR.

• Exposure made at the end of full expiration (patient breathes out completely and holds breath).

Central Ray

• Central Ray perpendicular to IR.

• Center Central Ray to level of T7 (approx. 18-20 cm below vertebra prominens).

• Center IR to CR (top of IR approximately 4–5 cm above shoulders).

• Collimate on four sides to outer chest margins.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Both lung fields included from apices to costophrenic angles.

✓ Diaphragm elevated to high position (fewer than 10 posterior ribs shown above diaphragm, usually 8–9).

✓ Small pneumothorax or air-trapping clearly visualized compared to inspiration view.

✓ No rotation: sternoclavicular joints equidistant from vertebral spine.

✓ Scapulae projected outside of the lung fields.
