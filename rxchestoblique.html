Chest Oblique

Oblique Chest Radiographic Projection (RAO / LAO / RPO / LPO)

Position Images

Examination Information

The oblique chest projections demonstrate lung fields, pulmonary structures, heart, and great vessels without superimposition. RAO and LAO views are commonly used for cardiac and pulmonary evaluations.

Body Part Chest / Thorax
Projection Oblique (RAO / LAO / RPO / LPO)
IR Size 35 × 43 cm
SID 180 cm
kVp 110 – 125
mAs 4 – 8

Patient Position

• Patient standing upright or seated erect facing IR (for RAO/LAO) or away from IR (for RPO/LPO).

• Rotate patient's body 45° (or 60° for heart/great vessels in LAO) relative to IR.

• Arm nearest IR placed on hip or supporting bar; opposite arm raised out of field.

• Center thoracic cavity to vertical grid midline.

• Exposure made on full inspiration (end of second deep breath).

Central Ray

• Central ray perpendicular to IR.

• Center Central Ray to level of T7 (approx. 18-20 cm below vertebra prominens).

• Collimate to outer margins of chest walls.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Both lung fields included from apices to costophrenic angles.

✓ Maximum area of lung field furthest from IR shown (for anterior obliques - RAO shows left lung, LAO shows right lung).

✓ Heart and great vessels clear of spine (especially in 60° LAO).

✓ Ribs and pulmonary structures demonstrated without excessive superimposition.

✓ Sharp bony detail and clear vascular markings across lung fields.
