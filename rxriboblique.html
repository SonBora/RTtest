Ribs Oblique (Axial / Oblique)

Axial/Oblique Radiographic Projection for Axillary Ribs (RPO / LPO / RAO / LAO)

Position Images

Examination Information

The oblique rib projection elongates and projects the axillary portion of the ribs free of superimposition. Posterior obliques (RPO/LPO) demonstrate the side dependent (closest to IR), whereas anterior obliques (RAO/LAO) demonstrate the side elevated (farthest from IR).

Body Part Ribs / Thoracic Cage
Projection Axillary / Oblique Ribs (AP/PA Oblique)
IR Size 35 × 43 cm (Lengthwise)
SID 100 cm
kVp 70 – 80 (Upper) / 75 – 85 (Lower)
mAs 10 – 20

Patient Position

• Patient standing erect or recumbent/supine depending on patient condition and upper vs. lower rib protocol.

• Rotate body 45 degrees towards affected side for Posterior Oblique (RPO/LPO demonstrates side down).

• Raise arm of affected side and rest hand on head; opposite arm placed across abdomen or on hip.

• Align mid-way between lateral border of rib cage and sternum/spine to midline of grid.

• Respiration: Full inspiration for upper ribs (1st–9th) or full expiration for lower ribs (8th–12th).

Central Ray

• Central Ray perpendicular to Image Receptor.

• Upper Ribs: Center CR to level of T7 (approx. 18–20 cm below vertebra prominens).

• Lower Ribs: Center CR to level midway between xiphoid process and lower rib margin (T12/L1).

• Center IR to CR (top of IR 4 cm above shoulders for upper ribs; bottom of IR at iliac crest for lower ribs).

• Collimate tightly to area of interest.

Image Evaluation Criteria

✓ Axillary portion of ribs projected elongated and free of superimposition with the vertebral column.

✓ Correct respiration demonstrated (above diaphragm for upper ribs; below diaphragm for lower ribs).

✓ 45-degree rotation clearly verified: spine rotated away from affected axillary rib zone.

✓ Optimum contrast and sharpness to reveal subtle cortical fractures or lesions.
